Privacy notice

Last updated 12 November 2026 · version 2

This is a plain-language statement written by the project's author. It is not legal advice and has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

The short version

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no ads, no analytics, no tracking pixels and no cookies.
  • The app is a set of files served by GitHub Pages. What you save (settings, saved places, journeys) stays on your device and is never sent to the author, who could not read it if they wanted to.
  • The author runs one small server: a relay that passes on İstanbul's live bus positions (section 4). It is asked only while you have an İstanbul bus line open, it is told only the line number, and it keeps nothing that identifies you.
  • Like any website, using the app means your device contacts the services in section 4 (for map tiles, weather and so on), and they can see your IP address and browser details. We chose services that need no sign-in and no key.
  • Two optional extras send what you typed, or the start and end of a walk, to other services. Both are off until you say yes (section 5).
  • You can erase everything the app stored on your device: Settings, Search and privacy, Delete all my data on this device.

1. Who is responsible

The operator is the author of TurkMaps, an individual (GitHub: Hero4mohamed). This is a personal project, not a company, and has no postal address. Contact: mohamedyounis248@gmail.com. "We" on this page means that author.

2. What the app keeps on your device

Your settings, the places you set as Home and Work, recent and starred journeys, followed lines, your travel passport figures, an optional API key, a short log of recent faults, and offline copies of the app and of map tiles you viewed. All of it is in your browser's own storage and none of it is sent to us. The full list, with when each item is written, is on the Cookies and stored data page.

3. How the app works, in one paragraph

The network, stations, timetables and the places list you search in are files that ship with the app, so searching and planning mostly happen on your device without asking anyone. The carriages you see moving are a simulation drawn on your device. What does reach other services is listed next.

4. Who your device talks to

Every service the app can contact is listed below, and the page's own security policy (its Content-Security-Policy) forbids contacting anything else. None of them is given an account, a key or an identifier by us. One of them, the TurkMaps relay, is run by us and its row says what it keeps; we have no access to what the others log.

5. Your choice about online look-ups

The first time you use the app (and after a material change to this notice) it asks one question: allow online look-ups, or keep everything on your device. The two buttons are the same size and either is fine. Until you answer, they are off.

When on, three things go to services run by others: the text you type into a place search when the list on your device finds too little (Photon), "what is near here" searches (Photon, sending the coordinates searched around), and the start and end of the walking parts of a planned journey (the OSRM server). If one of those points is your own position, that position is sent. You can change the choice at any time in Settings, Search and privacy.

Your location. The app asks your browser for your position only when you press a button that needs it ("my location", nearest stops, live journey), and your browser asks you first. The position is used on your device. It leaves the device only in the cases above, and only if you allowed online look-ups.

6. Notifications

If you follow a line and allow notifications, the app shows a notification on your device when a notice appears for that line. It is created on your device from notices the app downloads; there is no push server and nothing is sent about you. To stop, tap the 🔔 Following button on the line again, or turn notifications off in your browser's site settings.

7. Reports and e-mail

Report a problem opens a new public GitHub page with a draft already typed in: the recent fault messages, the app version, the city, the language, the screen size, whether you are online, and your browser's identification text. Nothing leaves your device until you press submit on GitHub, you can edit or delete any of it first, and what you post there is public. You need a GitHub account to post. The app never sends e-mails, text messages or marketing, and has no mailing list, so there is nothing to unsubscribe from.

8. Children

The app collects no personal data from anyone, of any age, and has no accounts, so it needs no age check or parental consent. If a child uses it, a parent or guardian may prefer to leave online look-ups off, which is the default.

9. Your rights

We hold no personal data about you, so there is nothing for us to look up, correct or delete: the data on your device is yours and the delete button removes it. For what the services in section 4 log (such as IP addresses) contact them through the links above. Data-protection laws such as Turkey's KVKK and the EU and UK GDPR give you rights over personal data and the right to complain to the supervisory authority; if you have any question or concern, write to us first at mohamedyounis248@gmail.com and we will answer.

10. Changes to this notice

If this notice changes in a way that matters, the date and version above change and the app asks its question again. Older versions are kept in the project's public source history.
